IMPLICATION FOR THE STUDY
================================================================================

The original finding is VALID, but with an important nuance:

CORRECT INTERPRETATION:
"Among AI companion users who exhibit any anthropomorphizing language in their 
discussions, teenagers show higher PEAK anthropomorphization than adults 
(d = 0.11, p < 0.0001)."

This is actually a MORE MEANINGFUL comparison because:
- We're comparing apples to apples (users who do anthropomorphize)
- The research question is about HOW users anthropomorphize, not WHETHER they do
- Many users discuss AI companions technically without anthropomorphizing

HOWEVER, the finding should be contextualized:
- 61% of users show no detectable anthropomorphization
- Adults are proportionally more represented among anthropomorphizers
- The effect is small (d = 0.11) though statistically robust

FINAL ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

The main finding (d = +0.11) IS robust for PREDICTED age.

However, the self-declared age validation raises a critical question:
- Does the predictor classify users based on actual age, OR
- Does it classify users based on behavior patterns that ALSO correlate with anthro?

If the age predictor partly captures "anthropomorphizing behavior" as an age signal,
there could be circularity in the finding.

RECOMMENDATION: Discuss this limitation in the paper. The effect is real for
predicted age groups, but may not generalize to self-declared age.
