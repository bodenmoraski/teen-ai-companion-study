================================================================================
INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

1. TEENS DO ANTHROPOMORPHIZE MORE - but the effect is small (Cohen's d = 0.11)

2. The effect is most visible in PEAK expressions - teens don't anthropomorphize
   more on average, but they're more likely to have intense anthropomorphizing
   moments.

3. YOUNG ADULTS show the LOWEST anthropomorphization, suggesting:
   - The effect may be developmental (younger teens most affected)
   - OR selection bias (young adults who use AI companions differ)
   - OR a generational effect (Gen Alpha vs Gen Z vs Millennials)

4. Activity explains SOME but not all of the effect:
   - Teens comment more (13.9 vs 10.1)
   - But even after normalization, effect remains significant
   - Proportion analysis is robust to activity differences

================================================================================
LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

1. Age classification still has ~16% error rate at high confidence
2. Observational data - cannot establish causation
3. Selection bias - only Reddit users who discuss AI companions
4. AnthroScore validity - 57% of users have zero score (silent majority)
5. Class imbalance - many more teens than non-teens in sample

================================================================================
CONCLUSION
================================================================================

We find STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT evidence that teens express higher peak
levels of anthropomorphization toward AI companions compared to non-teens.

The effect size is small (d=0.11) but robust across multiple analysis
approaches. Teens are 4-5 percentage points more likely to express high
anthropomorphization than non-teens.

Unexpectedly, young adults (19-25) show the LOWEST anthropomorphization,
suggesting a non-linear relationship with age that warrants further study.
